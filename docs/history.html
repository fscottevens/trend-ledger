Nothing is removed after the fact — the misses stay in alongside the hits.

How these numbers are calculated.
  • Which list counts: the last scan of each trading day — normally the 4:17pm ET post-close run. If that run failed, the latest scan from that day is used and marked “intraday”. A later weekend or holiday rerun never replaces a finished session.
  • Returns: price change from the close of the session a stock was listed to the close 1, 5, 10 and 20 trading sessions later, and to the latest close (“to date”). The S&P 500 figure is SPY over exactly the same windows. Price returns only — dividends excluded for both.
  • Averaging: every appearance counts, so a stock listed ten sessions running is counted ten times, each from its own starting close. Tick New entries only to count a stock only on sessions where it was not on either list the session before.
  • What it leaves out: no trading costs, slippage, taxes, stops or position sizing. The windows overlap, the sample is short, and a few months of one market regime say little about another. Sessions before September 29, 2026 have no daily % change because of a since-fixed data issue; their prices and rankings are unaffected.
